SCAM WATCH

Trust is the target.

The strongest scams do not look suspicious at first. They borrow the identity, urgency or authority of something you already recognise.

THE BIG WARNING

Stop. Verify. Then pay.

If a message asks for money, an OTP, a PIN, a password, remote access or an urgent transfer, treat the request as unverified until you independently confirm it. A familiar logo or convincing phone call is not proof.

India: if you have already experienced financial cyber fraud, report it quickly through the official 1930 helpline and National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal.

FINORA SECURITY DESK

Protect your money.

One practical security guide covering the most common pressure tactics used in modern financial fraud.

01

Use the five-second rule

When a message creates panic, urgency or excitement, do not act immediately. Scammers want you to make a decision before you have time to check the story. Put the phone down, breathe and verify the request through a separate official channel.

02

Never believe guaranteed returns

High returns with little or no risk are a classic warning sign. A polished investment dashboard, a WhatsApp group full of profit screenshots or a confident “expert” does not prove that an investment is genuine. Check the legal identity and registration of the intermediary using the relevant regulator's official information.

03

Do not pay to unlock your own money

Fraudulent investment platforms may display imaginary profits and then demand a tax, withdrawal fee, verification deposit or security payment before allowing a withdrawal. Sending more money usually increases the loss. Stop and independently investigate the platform instead.

04

Read every UPI screen

A UPI PIN authorises a payment. Never enter your PIN because someone says it is required to receive a refund, prize or payment. Read the name, amount and transaction type before approving anything.

05

End suspicious “digital arrest” calls

Criminals can impersonate police, customs, courts, regulators and other authorities. They may use official-looking documents and frightening language. Do not transfer money because someone claims your account is linked to a case. End the call and contact the organisation through independently found official details.

06

Verify urgent boss requests

If a manager, director or family member suddenly requests a bank transfer, gift card, cryptocurrency payment or change to bank details, use another known contact method. Do not rely on replying to the same account that sent the request.

07

Be careful with unexpected files

I4C has warned about malicious files disguised as account statements and regulatory communications. Do not open unexpected ZIP archives or executable files merely because the filename contains a familiar institution's name. Verify first.

08

Protect your email and banking accounts

Use strong, unique passwords and two-step verification where available. Keep devices and apps updated. Review logged-in sessions. Never share OTPs, PINs, card security codes or passwords with callers, even when the caller sounds official.

09

Check the real website

Scammers can copy logos, colours and layouts. Appearance is not authentication. Instead of tapping a suspicious link, type or independently locate the organisation's official website and navigate from there.

10

Do not let screenshots make the decision

Profit screenshots, payment receipts and “successful withdrawal” images can be fabricated. Treat them as claims, not evidence. Verify the underlying service and transaction independently.

11

Be sceptical of recovery agents

After a scam, victims are sometimes targeted again by people claiming they can recover the money for an upfront fee. Do not send additional money to an unknown person promising guaranteed recovery. Use official reporting and your bank or payment provider.

12

Report quickly if money has moved

Speed matters. Contact your bank or payment provider immediately and use India's official 1930 cyber-fraud helpline and National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal. Preserve transaction IDs, screenshots, phone numbers, emails, URLs and chat history. Do not delete evidence.
